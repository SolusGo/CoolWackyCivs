# The Dual Order - strategy guide

[Player handbook](../StrategyGuide.md) · [Matchups](../Matchups.md) · [Full mechanics](../../DualOrder/README.md)

**Leader:** Grandmaster Severin  
**Playstyle:** Faith-supported military infrastructure and defensive campaigning  
**Suggested victories:** Domination; Culture/Diplomacy through a strong religious economy

Build the religious and military infrastructure that makes trained units durable, retain positive Faith income, and choose conflicts your army can actually manage. Extra wars increase a capped modifier but also create real military liabilities.

## Opening priorities

Establish food, defense and Faith before pursuing multiple commitments. Plan at least one city with both military-training and religious infrastructure so normally trained units receive Zeal.

## Develop the engine

Use Halls of Concordance to strengthen training cities. Pair ranged damage with Templar finishing attacks and protect any religious unit providing adjacency support. During Golden Ages, complete useful military production.

## Convert the advantage

Hold supply lines and friendly-territory positions while converting production into a coherent army. Keep a realistic peace plan; nominal Balance Pressure is not worth losing cities or your Faith-producing economy.

## Pros

- Paired infrastructure supports Faith and Production while enabling Zeal.
- Trained veterans retain a useful territorial strength and kill-healing package.
- Golden Ages and Halls create effective military preparation windows.

## Cons and opportunity costs

- The strongest setup requires investment in two infrastructure groups.
- Balance Pressure needs positive Faith income and has a war-count cap.
- Religious support units are vulnerable, and territorial conditions limit some bonuses.

## Playing against this civ

- Threaten the Faith and training centres rather than trading endlessly into prepared positions.
- Draw forces away from favourable territory and protect wounded units from Templar finishes.
- Avoid creating extra fronts that benefit Severin without applying meaningful pressure.

## Common mistakes

- Declaring token wars only to raise the pressure indicator.
- Training in a city missing one half of the Zeal requirement.
- Treating a General as a substitute for the Templar's specified religious adjacency.
