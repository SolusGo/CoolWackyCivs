# The Kingdoms - strategy guide

[Player handbook](../StrategyGuide.md) · [Matchups](../Matchups.md) · [Full mechanics](../../TheKingdoms/README.md)

**Leader:** The Throne  
**Playstyle:** Political stability, controlled expansion and named guards  
**Suggested victories:** Flexible; Science/Culture from stability or supported Domination

Keep local Houses and the succession manageable while building a conventional empire. The Crown's military and economic plans work best when it can meet reasonable demands without empowering every claimant indiscriminately.

## Opening priorities

Develop food, Gold and defensible cities before adding more political obligations. Read House personalities and loyalty. Walls provide useful stability support; retain money for demands rather than committing the whole treasury to expansion.

## Develop the engine

Resolve demands using their actual current requirements. Evaluate Estates, Charters and authority choices for their costs and political consequences. Appoint King's Guards deliberately and preserve the useful identities already serving the realm.

## Convert the advantage

Enter succession with stable institutions and a viable treasury. If civil war occurs, choose backing consciously and protect the economy through its penalties. Resume a normal victory programme after reunification rather than accepting permanent crisis management.

## Pros

- Stable institutions and local Walls support a durable realm.
- Named Guards reward careful appointments and veteran preservation.
- House management gives several levers for solving political problems.

## Cons and opportunity costs

- Low Stability can turn succession into a costly civil war.
- Demands and appeasement compete with construction, Gold and military priorities.
- Supporting powerful personalities can create later political liabilities.

## Playing against this civ

- Apply economic and military pressure when demands or succession are already stretching the realm.
- Threaten several important cities so Guards and garrisons cannot solve every problem.
- Avoid assuming a civil war creates a new ordinary rival civilization; exploit its actual internal penalties.

## Common mistakes

- Ignoring a feasible demand until its expiry penalty arrives.
- Treating a claimant score as a guaranteed succession probability.
- Expanding or campaigning without money and stability for the next political cycle.
