**Leader:** Trent Rou'ls, the Unbodied
**Civilization:** The Rou'ls Ascendancy
**Adjective:** Rou'ls
**Capital:** Trentaal
**Preferred Victory:** Domination / Science
**Secondary Victory:** Diplomatic
**Start Bias:** None
**Religion:** None preferred

> **“The flesh is a coat. Wear it well, and pass it on.”**

Also called **the Wearers**, **the Passengers**, and **the Second-Skin Choir**.

---

# Core Identity

The Rou'ls are an **attrition, possession, redeployment, and unit-preservation civilization**.

They are not intended to have the strongest army statistically.

Instead:

* Enemy deaths fuel **Anima**.
* Anima lets the Rou'ls replace casualties.
* Important military units can effectively **change bodies**.
* Veteran units are unusually difficult to permanently lose.
* Their empire becomes progressively harder to dislodge once wars begin.
* Their most powerful mechanics require units to actually die.

The Rou'ls therefore become substantially stronger in prolonged wars than they are during peaceful openings.

# Unique Ability

## **The Flesh Is a Coat**

The Rou'ls possess a unique resource called **Anima**.

### Harvest of Vessels

Whenever a Rou'ls military unit destroys an enemy military unit:

**Gain 1 Anima.**

Maximum:

**5 Anima**

Anima is displayed in a unique counter on the Top Panel.

### Nothing Is Lost

Whenever one of your non-civilian land units is killed, you may automatically spend:

**1 Anima**

to restore the unit's consciousness into a replacement body.

The destroyed unit is replaced in the nearest valid friendly city with:

* the same unit class where possible;
* the same level;
* the same experience;
* all valid promotions;
* **50% HP**.

The replacement cannot act until your next turn.

Units recreated this way do not trigger additional death-related Anima effects.

If you have no Anima, the unit dies normally.

### Limits

Nothing Is Lost does not work on:

* Settlers
* Workers
* Great People
* Religious units
* civilian units
* units already recreated through Nothing Is Lost that turn

Maximum:

**2 transmigrations per turn.**

This prevents mass infantry armies from becoming functionally immortal.

# Unique Civilization Mechanic

# TRANSMIGRATION

Once every **8 turns**, the Rou'ls may activate **TRANSMIGRATION**.

Select two eligible Rou'ls military units.

The units exchange their positions.

### Requirements

Both units:

* must belong to the Rou'ls;
* cannot be embarked;
* cannot be aircraft;
* cannot be Fighters;
* cannot currently be inside enemy territory;
* cannot occupy a tile containing an enemy unit;
* must be able to legally exist on their destination tile.

Land units may only exchange with land units.

Naval units may only exchange with naval units.

### Cost

Base cost:

**2 Anima**

### After Transmigration

Both units:

* lose all remaining movement;
* cannot attack during that turn.

### Strategic Purpose

You can keep:

* veteran units defending your homeland;
* fresh troops near the front;
* siege equipment in reserve;
* wounded units away from danger;

and suddenly exchange them when required.

A highly promoted unit can effectively leave one body behind and appear somewhere completely different.

# Unique Building

# **Somatic Lattice**

Replaces: **Hospital**

The Somatic Lattice stores biological templates, replacement neural architecture, and empty bodies.

### Normal Hospital Effects

Retains the Hospital's normal Food effects.

### Additional Effects

**+2 Science**

**+2 Production**

Whenever a military unit trained in this city reaches completion:

**25% chance to gain 1 Anima**

Cannot exceed the normal 5 Anima maximum.

---

### Passive Anima Generation

If you currently possess **0 Anima**:

Every Somatic Lattice has a chance to generate one replacement consciousness reserve.

Once every **10 turns**, if you have zero Anima:

**Gain 1 Anima**

regardless of how many Somatic Lattices you own.

This gives the Rou'ls a fallback during peace without allowing infinite stockpiling.

# Unique Once-Per-Game Ability

# **THE GREAT MIGRATION**

Available after entering the **Modern Era**.

Requires:

**7 Anima**

Consumes all 7.

Once per game.

Select an enemy military unit within **3 tiles of one of your military units**.

The target must:

* not be a Great Person;
* not be civilian;
* not be a unique one-per-player special unit;
* not be a nuclear weapon;
* not be an aircraft.

The target is destroyed.

You immediately create the **Rou'ls equivalent of its unit class** on its tile.

The new unit begins with:

**75% HP**

and receives:

**50% of the destroyed unit's accumulated XP**, capped at **60 XP**.

The unit cannot move or attack until the following turn.

### Example

Enemy Mechanized Infantry is consumed.

You receive a Rou'ls Mechanized Infantry.

Enemy Destroyer is consumed.

You receive a Rou'ls Destroyer.

Enemy Tank is consumed.

You receive a Rou'ls Tank.

The Rou'ls have not captured the machinery.

They have captured the **role**, copied the vessel and placed something else inside.

# Civilization Weakness

The Rou'ls are deliberately mediocre before casualties begin occurring.

Early-game disadvantages:

* No Ancient/Classical unique unit.
* No economic yield steroid.
* No direct Food bonus.
* No free technologies.
* No free Settlers.
* No Production acceleration.
* Anima is difficult to generate during peace.
* TRANSMIGRATION costs precious Anima.
* Nothing Is Lost consumes the same resource.
* Their UB arrives relatively late.
* Their strongest military tools require significant infrastructure.

This reproduces the TI4 weakness:

> **“Their abilities do nothing until units start dying.”**

# How the Civilization Plays

## Ancient Era

Play relatively conventionally.

The Rou'ls are not supposed to explode out of the gate.

Your priorities are:

* establish 3–4 cities;
* obtain strategic resources;
* build a respectable military;
* prepare for future Anima generation.

Avoid unnecessary casualties because Anima generation initially relies primarily on enemy deaths.

---

# Classical–Medieval

Small wars begin favouring you.

Every enemy unit destroyed becomes potential survivability later.

You may begin deliberately taking favourable skirmishes specifically to stockpile Anima.

At five Anima, the Rou'ls suddenly become far more dangerous.

---

# Renaissance

The Hollowhound arrives.

This is the first major Rou'ls power spike.

Veteran Hollowhounds are extremely difficult to permanently remove because they receive one free rebirth before requiring Anima.

Rou'ls armies begin developing unusually experienced cores.

---

# Industrial

Somatic Lattices create a sustainable Anima economy.

At this stage, killing Rou'ls units without finishing the war quickly becomes problematic.

A veteran unit killed outside your empire may reappear several turns later defending Trentaal.

---

# Modern

**THE GREAT MIGRATION** unlocks.

The Rou'ls gain their strongest offensive tempo tool.

Instead of merely preserving their own army, they can erase an important enemy unit and immediately materialize its equivalent.

---

# Late Game

The Rou'ls become horrifying in wars of attrition.

Their advantage is not:

> “My Infantry has 20% more strength.”

It is:

> “You have killed this Infantry three times.”

# AI Handling

The civilization remains AI-compatible.

### Anima

AI automatically gains and spends Anima.

Priority for Nothing Is Lost:

1. highly promoted units;
2. siege units;
3. ranged units;
4. Hollowhounds;
5. expensive modern units;
6. other units.

### TRANSMIGRATION

For humans:

Use a dedicated UI button.

For AI:

The Lua controller evaluates units during its turn and attempts exchanges when:

* a strong unit is defending an area with no nearby enemies;
* another front is currently threatened;
* an injured veteran can be exchanged for a healthy reserve unit;
* a major offensive is occurring.

If good AI positioning logic proves unreliable, the AI can instead receive a simplified hidden version:

Every 8 turns, one endangered military unit may teleport to the nearest friendly city for 2 Anima.

The human player keeps the full swap mechanic.

# Civilopedia — Civilization

## The Rou'ls Ascendancy

The Rou'ls were not born as a species.

They were once a research consortium on the world of Trentaal whose scholars attempted to answer one of civilization's oldest questions: could consciousness survive the body?

Their first successful experiment was neither soldier nor ruler.

It was a dying companion.

An aged quadruped whose joints had failed beyond medicine was transferred into a healthy replacement body. It awakened confused, joyous, and apparently entirely itself.

The founder reportedly wept for six hours.

Then he published.

The consequences reshaped Trentaal.

Within centuries, biological death became increasingly optional. Within millennia, the concept of an original body had virtually disappeared. Bodies became vessels, inheritance became consciousness, and identity ceased to depend upon flesh.

Ten thousand years later, no known citizen of the Ascendancy inhabits the body in which they were born.

Their soldiers do not fear death.

Their generals change bodies between campaigns.

Their greatest veterans have died hundreds of times.

And somewhere aboard the warship known as Buddy, Everlasting, there still resides the same uncomplicated and endlessly enthusiastic consciousness whose first transmigration began the Ascendancy itself.

To the Rou'ls, nothing is truly lost.

It has merely moved.

---

# Civilopedia — Strategy

The Rou'ls Ascendancy excels in wars where both civilizations suffer repeated casualties.

Destroying enemy units generates Anima, which can then be spent to preserve experienced Rou'ls units that would otherwise die.

Because reconstructed units retain their experience and promotions, the Rou'ls can gradually accumulate a highly experienced army even during costly wars.

TRANSMIGRATION allows experienced units stationed far from the battlefield to exchange places with units closer to the front, giving the Rou'ls exceptional strategic mobility.

However, both TRANSMIGRATION and unit resurrection draw upon the same limited Anima pool. Spending aggressively on mobility may leave your army unable to recover from casualties.

The civilization is weakest during peaceful openings. Without units dying, Anima is scarce and most Rou'ls abilities provide little economic benefit.

Choose wars carefully.

The Rou'ls do not necessarily win the first battle.

They win because they can keep fighting the fifth.

# Dawn of Man

**The flesh is a coat, Trent Rou'ls.**

Long ago your people discovered that death was not an ending, but a failure of transportation.

The first passenger was merely a dying companion whose failing body could no longer carry him. When he awoke within another, the distinction between body and self was shattered forever.

Now ten thousand years have passed.

Your citizens wear forms they were never born into.

Your veterans have died more times than your enemies have fought wars.

Your fleets carry consciousness from vessel to vessel, and the joyful passenger aboard Buddy, Everlasting still sails beside you as he has since before ancient empires fell into dust.

Yet beyond Trentaal dwell peoples who still believe flesh defines them.

Show them otherwise.

When armies fall, wear new bodies.

When fleets burn, take new vessels.

When the universe believes the Rou'ls destroyed...

**return.**
