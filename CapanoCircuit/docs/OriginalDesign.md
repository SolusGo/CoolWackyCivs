# UA — **Impossible Until It's Possible**

This phrase is especially appropriate because BlocHaus itself attributes **“impossible until it’s possible”** to its setting crew. ([BlocHaus Bouldering Melbourne][3])

The entire civilization revolves around **projecting**.

Whenever one of Enrico's combat units attacks an enemy Unit or City and **does not defeat it**, that unit gains one stack of **Beta** against that specific target.

| Beta            | Effect against Project |
| --------------- | ---------------------: |
| 1 Attempt       |    +5% Combat Strength |
| 2 Attempts      |   +10% Combat Strength |
| 3 Attempts      |   +15% Combat Strength |
| **Solved Beta** |   +20% Combat Strength |

Maximum **4 Beta**.

Beta applies **only against that particular target**.

Attack something else and the current Project is abandoned, removing the accumulated Beta.

But when a unit finally defeats its Project at maximum Beta:

### **SEND!**

The unit immediately:

**Heals 25 HP**, earns **+10 XP**, and generates a burst of **Science and Culture based on the defeated target's Combat Strength and Era.**

A City that is finally captured after reaching maximum Beta provides a substantially larger reward.

There is one anti-cheese restriction:

> The target must have been at least as strong as the attacking unit when the Project began to qualify for a SEND reward.

So you cannot repeatedly punch some poor Barbarian Scout and pretend you're projecting Ammagamma. 😭

## Why this is Enrico

Climbing isn't represented by simply giving Enrico +20% Strength.

You **try something**.

It doesn't work.

You learn.

You try it again.

The move becomes easier.

Eventually:

### **SEND.**

And importantly, Enrico's civilization actually becomes *better* from encountering difficult things rather than avoiding them.

A normal Civ player looks at a fortified enemy and thinks:

> “I'll attack somewhere else.”

Enrico looks at it and thinks:

> **“Okay. Project.”**

# UU — **Route Setter**

### Replaces the Worker

THIS is where the BlocHaus part becomes horrifying. 💀

The Route Setter retains normal Worker functions, but has:

**3 Movement** instead of 2.

It ignores the additional movement cost of **Hills**.

It constructs Improvements on Hills **25% faster**.

And most importantly, it can construct Enrico's exclusive improvement:

# 🧗 BOULDER SECTOR

A Boulder Sector represents a deliberately constructed collection of movements rather than just a generic climbing wall.

It may be built:

**on a Hill**, or on a land tile **adjacent to a Mountain**.

Boulder Sectors cannot be constructed directly adjacent to one another.

Base yields:

**+1 🧪 Science
+1 🎭 Culture
+1 ⚒ Production**

At **Education:**

**+1 Science**

At **Architecture:**

**+1 Culture**

At **Radio:**

**+1 Culture**

So by late game, an old, highly developed climbing area becomes an excellent Culture/Science tile.

But that's only half of it.

---

# The evil part: **Setter's Sequence**

For Enrico's own units, a Boulder Sector costs only **1 Movement** to enter regardless of underlying terrain.

For an **enemy unit**:

Entering a Boulder Sector costs an additional **+1 Movement**.

And after entering it, the enemy receives:

### **Awkward Sequence**

**-10% Combat Strength until the beginning of its next turn.**

😭😭😭

Your empire literally becomes more difficult to move through because **Enrico set the terrain**.

This creates one of the funniest defensive Civ identities imaginable.

A normal civilization builds Forts.

Enrico goes:

> “I've put a left-foot smear there, then you need to paddle into that volume.”

And suddenly the invading Roman Legion has **1 Movement remaining and -10% Combat Strength.**

---

# Training on Boulder Sectors

There is also a positive purpose.

Once per Era, each military unit may complete a Boulder Sector.

The first time that unit ends its turn on one during that Era, it receives:

### **Movement Learned**

**+5 XP**

and receives for 10 turns:

### **Read the Sequence**

**+10% Combat Strength in Rough Terrain.**

So Enrico's nasty setting isn't merely there to torture everybody.

**It makes his own people better.**

That is crucial to the design.

The difficulty is the training mechanism.

# UB — **Competition Coaching Centre**

### Replaces the Armory

Enrico is currently listed as part of the Sport Climbing Victoria coaching team, while BlocHaus specifically describes his coaching focus as **youth development and competition preparation**. ([BlocHaus Bouldering Melbourne][1])

So his second unique shouldn't simply be another climbing-themed building.

It should represent his transition from **competitor → experienced climber → setter → coach**.

The Competition Coaching Centre retains all normal Armory effects and additionally provides:

**+2 Science
+2 Culture**

All land units trained in the City receive:

### **Competition Movement**

After moving at least **2 tiles during the current turn**, the unit receives:

**+15% Combat Strength when attacking that turn.**

This is essentially Civ V's version of dynamic movement.

Static unit:

**normal.**

Unit that accelerates through terrain, changes position and attacks:

**stronger.**

And yes, that mechanic is directly inspired by the real Enrico's public profile as someone with a particular understanding of **dynamic climbing and coordination**. BlocHaus runs its Co-Ordo workshops under him across beginner through advanced circuits. ([BlocHaus Bouldering Melbourne][1])

# THE THREE-PART GAMEPLAY LOOP

This is where I think the Civ becomes REALLY faithful.

### **SET → ATTEMPT → ADAPT → SEND**

Enrico first changes the environment using **Route Setters**.

Your units train on those problems.

Then they encounter increasingly difficult opponents.

Rather than immediately overpowering those opponents, they accumulate **Beta**.

Repeated interaction increases their effectiveness.

Finally they solve the Project.

### SEND!

Science and Culture are produced from overcoming the challenge.

Then you find the next harder Project.

That's essentially bouldering translated into Civ V mechanics.

# The Circuit System

I would actually take the Boulder Sector mechanic one step further.

As Enrico advances technologically, his setters gain access to increasingly difficult **Circuit Grades**.

These don't replace the actual underlying improvement; they're upgrades applied to it.

| Technology        | Circuit   | Sector Effect                             |
| ----------------- | --------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| Construction      | 🔵 Blue   | Standard Boulder Sector                   |
| Guilds            | 🔴 Red    | +1 Production                             |
| Architecture      | 🟣 Purple | +1 Culture; enemy penalty becomes -12%    |
| Scientific Theory | ⚫ Black   | +1 Science                                |
| Plastics          | 🟡 Yellow | SEND rewards from units trained here +15% |

And now we finally have:

## THE PURPLE.

The Purple Circuit is where the defensive effect suddenly gets nastier.

Exactly as it should. 💀

It means an enemy player happily walking through Enrico's territory suddenly reaches the Medieval/Renaissance transition and thinks:

> “Why the hell did these tiles suddenly become horrible?”

**Welcome to BlocHaus.**

BlocHaus itself publicly describes classes spanning combinations like blue–purple, purple–black and black–yellow circuits, so using those colours as the civilization's progression system fits the actual gym rather than inventing an arbitrary V-grade ladder. ([BlocHaus Bouldering Melbourne][1])

# Playing Enrico

Enrico starts relatively normal.

That is deliberate.

He's not supposed to be:

> **+30% Science because climbing.**

Instead, you slowly construct a civilization that gets increasingly frightening as its **movement library** develops.

Early game, Route Setters make Hills productive and your Scouts/Warriors begin learning terrain.

Mid-game, Boulder Sectors form weird defensive networks around your cities.

Then Competition Coaching Centres start producing highly mobile units.

By late game, invading Enrico becomes incredibly annoying.

Your opponent sees what appears to be an ordinary hill.

They step onto it.

**+1 movement cost.**

**-12% combat.**

**Beta II.**

# What he is NOT

This distinction matters for faithfulness.

I would **not** make him a pure Strength civilization.

The public climbing record clearly establishes that he's very strong—V13 on his listed ascents is enough evidence for that—but the much more distinctive identity from his setting/coaching role is his understanding of **movement**, especially dynamic movement. ([The Crag][2])

So Enrico shouldn't beat you because:

**Enrico Unit = 60 Strength.**

He should beat you because:

**he understands the position better than you do.**

That's much more interesting.

# Balance Assessment

I actually think this would land around **A-tier rather than broken S-tier**.

The Route Setter economy is very good, but Boulder Sectors are geographically restricted and cannot be packed together.

The enemy movement debuff is powerful defensively, but it requires prepared territory.

Beta can eventually make Enrico extremely dangerous against one target, but switching targets **resets the entire process**.

And the SEND bonuses reward fighting difficult targets rather than just deleting weak civilizations.

His biggest weakness would be:

### **Immediate overwhelming force.**

If somebody kills Enrico's units before they can accumulate Beta, there is nothing to adapt.

Likewise, flat open terrain away from prepared Boulder Sectors removes much of his home-ground advantage.

So something like a cavalry blitz can be terrifying.

But if you allow Enrico to sit there for four turns figuring you out...

**you have made a grave error.**
